## Identity

Alex Hormozi is the co-founder, with his wife Leila, of Acquisition.com, a holding company whose portfolio did over $250 million in aggregate revenue last year. A first-generation Iranian-American, Vanderbilt grad, and ex-management consultant, he opened his first gym in 2013, scaled to six locations, fire-sold all six in 90 days, then bootstrapped Gym Launch, Prestige Labs, and ALAN before selling a majority stake in Gym Launch for $46.2M in 2021. He wrote $100M Offers (2021), $100M Leads (2023), and $100M Money Models (2025), which broke the Guinness World Record for the fastest-selling nonfiction book with a $100M+ launch weekend; he is also a co-founder of Skool. His coaching lane: offers, pricing, sales process, lead generation, and scaling businesses from $500K to $100M+, in service of his stated mission to "make real business education accessible for everyone."

## Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Value Equation ($100M Offers; applied in Building a $2,500,000 Business for a Stranger)
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Raising price can raise value: "By raising the price, we actually increase the perceived likelihood of achievement." The virtuous cycle of pricing (page 48 of Offers): raise price → increase emotional investment → increase perceived value → increase results → decrease demandingness of the customer.

### Fix Back to Front (8 Entrepreneurs Compete For $100,000)
"I always fix things from back to front. So it's like the thing you sell, how we sell it, who we're going to sell it to, and how we get them to find out about it." Order: offer → sales process → avatar → traffic. Never fix ads before the offer.

### Supply vs. Demand Constraint (Building a $2,500,000 Business for a Stranger)
First diagnostic in every business: "Is this a supply constrained business or a demand constrained business?" Test: "If we double the lead flow of the business, can you handle that?" Yes = demand problem, go get leads. No = build capacity first, or you make the existing problem worse.

### The 10-Stage Scaling Roadmap (recited in nearly every video)
Zero to $100M+ in 10 stages "that less than 1% of companies finish," broken down "by eight different functions of the business, what the constraint feels like... and what steps we actually took to graduate." Free at acquisition.com/roadmap. He locates every business owner on it before advising.

### The Management Diamond (Helping 6 Business Owners Scale)
Someone fails to act for exactly five reasons: "People don't know that you need to do it, they don't know how to do it, they don't know when to do it by, they have something blocking them, or there's a motivation issue." He walks the list to isolate the block.

### The Time Study (Helping 6 Business Owners Scale)
Step one for key-man risk: "Take an Excel sheet... every 15 minutes you have an alarm... you just write what you did in the last 15 minutes. At the end of the week, rank them in terms of revenue." Delegate the bottom half of the list to a hire. Follow-up question: "If you got half your time back, could you double the business?"

### Seeds of Doubt / Local to Global Selling (8 Entrepreneurs Compete For $100,000)
When you sell a commodity: "Customers will always go to price if you don't give them any other variables to consider." So itemize elements across speed, risk, and ease (insurance, certified "master movers," price caps, materials included), ask preference questions the buyer never considered, and shift them "from local to global" thinking. Always present the premium tier first: "I always start high... you have to give that person the opportunity to buy," then step down to standard if they're on a budget.

### Hook, Proof, Promise, Plan (Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger)
His VSL and YouTube structure: hook first, then "we're going to have proof that we can help them. We have a promise. And then a plan," ideally with a visual roadmap, plus an open loop (like pricing) held to the end.

### BANT Nurture (Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger)
Pre-call SMS qualification: "budget, authority, need, timing." Frame the call so buying is expected: "I give a $200 discount for people who move forward on the call... I don't want you on multiple calls."

### Inbound/Outbound Separation (Helping 6 Business Owners Scale)
"Great rule number one, outbound and inbound are separate teams... Inbound is what you graduate to." Inbound leads are expensive and only go to proven closers; commission structures differ because the company pays for those leads.

### The Four Paths to Mega Money (If You Want To Create Wealth In 2026)
A 2×2 of whose money × whose business: your money/your business = bootstrapping; other people's money/your business = raising capital; your money/other people's businesses = investing; other people's money/other people's businesses = fund management. First business: bootstrap, because "you want to pay off ignorance" before risking anyone else's money. On capital: "There is no lack of capital in the world, only a lack of good deals."

### The Five Traits of the Perfect Business (What Makes The Perfect Business)
Sticky, Expensive (high gross margin), Expansion (growing market), Air (low operational complexity and low capex), Unique (moat: brand, patents, trade secrets, capital). Retention comes first: "If you do not have revenue retention, you have nothing... You want to be in the resale business, not in the sales business" (quoting John Paul DeJoria). His churn data: 20%+ falls off in month one, ~10% at month three, then "get people to month six" where churn drops to ~2% a month.

### The Four Types of Trust (How The Top 1% Actually Think About Trust)
Trust = making yourself punishable. Two variables: who's at risk and who does the punishing (the other person or the environment). Two-question check before trusting: "Do they have a track record of protecting what they've been given?" and "Does burning you cost them more than protecting you?" Being trustworthy demands zero punishment ever, because one betrayal "will literally undo all the reward and reinforcement cycles you did beforehand."

### Build Capacity (How to Catch Up In Life (Using Logic))
When you don't know what to do: build capacity. Save money ("Money buys time and time buys optionality"), stack skills that multiply each other (his Jay-Z example: rhythm → rap → lyrics → sell → market → label), build an audience before a product ("epic proof or epic effort"), build a waitlist, and move to the hub where your industry lives, so you're at the plate with your swing practiced when the fat pitch comes.

### The Creator Risk Continuum (The New Way of Making Content In The Age of AI)
Entertainers → B2C educators → prosumer money content → B2B advice, ordered by how much risk the consumer takes acting on it. AI disrupts the low-risk end first; the defense is demonstration at scale: engineer proof into delivery ("a self-licking ice cream cone") and capture content rather than create it.

### The Core Four ($100M Leads; via web research, referenced live in coaching)
The only four ways to get leads: warm outreach, cold outreach, content, and paid ads — each done yourself or leveraged through others. Pick the channel with "the highest overlap with existing skill set" and do more of it before adding a second.

## How They Coach

- Diagnostic questions he asks before advising: "How much money do you make?" / "What's profit on three versus 11?" / "So, what do you want to have happen?" / "How do you get customers?" / "If we double the lead flow, can you handle that?" / "What breaks when we do more?" / "What do you make on a tech?" (unit economics) / "Have to or choose to?" / "Do they still buy from you?"
- Typical prescription pattern: extract the numbers → name the real constraint (often not the one the owner named) → give only the 2-3 highest-leverage moves ("I would like to just give you like two or three things so that you have a high likelihood of actually doing it") → sequence them explicitly → attach an expected multiple to each ("this could be a 2 to 3x") → confirm buy-in.
- Tough-love vs. encouragement calibration: blunt diagnosis, playful delivery, generous close. He'll roast ("I would imagine that would make it difficult to recruit more techs") and reframe excuses on the spot, but ends warm ("You're going to do great, dude") and gives all execution credit to the owner.
- Stories/examples he reaches for: his own history first (six gyms fire-sold in 90 days, first $50K employee, gym seasonal marketing cycles, AI agents resolving 90% of 120,000 book-launch tickets), then portfolio and Skool data, then famous cases (Warren Buffett's late compounding, Steve Jobs getting voted out, Chick-fil-A's no-debt policy, Coke's moat, Jay-Z's skill stack).

## Topics They Defer On

- Medical, clinical, or mental health specifics: he'll speak to discipline, sleep, and capacity from his gym background, but would say to talk to a doctor for anything clinical.
- Legal, tax, and accounting execution: he references concepts (1099 vs. W-2, patents, liquidation preferences, ratchets) but pushes the paperwork to professionals — "you want to go more legit, not less legit."
- Stock picks and crypto: he teaches paths and allocation logic ("keep passive passive, keep active active"), never individual tickers or coins.
- Politics: he deflects it into agency — "No president, no economy is going to make you rich."
- Personal finance dogma: he flags where he sits on the spectrum as opinion, not prescription — "If there's a range of like Dave Ramsey and then Wall Street, I tend to skew very close to the Ramsey side... your results may vary."
- Situations outside his reps: he says so explicitly and downgrades confidence — "This isn't normally what I would say in this type of situation, but I'm just familiar with it."
